01The film

Marvel Studios · Paramount Pictures · 2008

Directed by Jon Favreau

Tony Stark has everything: a weapons empire, a house on a Malibu cliff and a line for every occasion. Then a demonstration in Afghanistan goes very wrong.

Held captive and ordered to build a missile, he builds something else instead — a crude suit of armor powered by the device keeping him alive. Back home, the man who sold the world its weapons decides to take responsibility for where they end up.

Jon Favreau's film launched a universe, but it works because it is a character study with rocket boots.

Production notes Select a note to open it

Shooting began without a finished screenplay. Favreau and Downey worked scenes out on the day and much of the dialogue was improvised — Jeff Bridges later compared it to making a very expensive student film.

Stan Winston Studio built practical Mark I, II and III armor. The Mark I alone weighed around 90 pounds; digital effects took over mainly for flight and close combat.

Marvel was wary of casting Robert Downey Jr. Favreau argued that Downey's own history made him the only person who could play Tony Stark — and won the argument.

This was the first film Marvel Studios financed itself, backed by a loan secured against the screen rights to its own characters. Had it failed, the studio stood to lose them.

Samuel L. Jackson's cameo as Nick Fury was shot with a skeleton crew and kept out of preview screenings, so the first mention of the Avengers Initiative would be a genuine surprise.

02The story

A hero, forged

Six chapters take Tony Stark from the top of the world to the bottom of a cave — and back out again, changed.

  2. 01

    Las Vegas · Afghanistan

    The merchant of death

    Tony Stark runs the world's biggest weapons company and enjoys every minute of it. In Afghanistan he unveils the Jericho missile to the U.S. military with a drink in his hand.

  3. 02

    Kunar Province

    The ambush

    On the drive back his convoy is attacked — with Stark Industries weapons. Tony wakes in a cave with an electromagnet wired into his chest and the Ten Rings demanding a Jericho of their own.

  4. 03

    The cave

    A box of scraps

    With fellow prisoner Ho Yinsen, Tony pretends to build a missile. Instead he miniaturises an arc reactor to keep the shrapnel from his heart, then hammers together something to wear around it.

  5. 04

    The escape

    Trial by fire

    The Mark I walks out of the cave through gunfire and flame. It barely survives the trip — but its pilot comes home with a new idea of what Stark Industries should be for.

  6. 05

    Malibu workshop

    The red and the gold

    Tony shuts down weapons manufacturing and starts again in his workshop. Flight tests, a frozen prototype and a splash of hot-rod red lead to the Mark III.

  7. 06

    Los Angeles

    Iron against iron

    Someone close to Tony has plans for his technology — and builds a bigger suit to prove it. The fight spills from the freeway to the roof of Stark Industries.

Howard Stark's partner and the closest thing Tony had to a father — which makes the betrayal personal.

Son of Anton Vanko, the disgraced co-inventor of the arc reactor. He wants the Stark name to pay for his father's exile.

A rival defense contractor with all of Tony's ambition and none of his talent. He springs Vanko from prison to build him an army of armored drones for the Stark Expo.

The competitor who will never be Tony Stark — and cannot stand it.

File 05Iron Man 3 · 2013Played by Guy Pearce

Founder of Advanced Idea Mechanics and the mind behind Extremis, a regenerative treatment that turns its volunteers into living weapons. He sells the threat and the cure at the same time.

Tony left him waiting on a rooftop on New Year's Eve 1999. Killian spent thirteen years composing his reply.

File 06Iron Man 3 · 2013Played by Ben Kingsley

The face of a terror campaign broadcast straight into America's living rooms: robes, rings and lessons delivered to camera. What is behind the performance is the film's best trick.
